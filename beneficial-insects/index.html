🌼 What to Plant to Bring the Good Bugs In

You do not have to buy bugs in a bag. Plant the right flowers. The good bugs already near you will move in on their own.

Good bugs do not show up for nothing. Most of them eat pollen or nectar as grown-ups. Their babies are the ones that eat pests. No flowers, no good bugs. Plant a few of the flowers below near your vegetables. You give the good bugs a reason to stay.

The 5 flower families that do the most work

Carrot family (Apiaceae)

Dill · fennel · caraway · cilantro/coriander · Queen Anne's lace · Bishop's flower

These flowers grow in flat clusters. Each cluster has hundreds of tiny blooms. Small wasps often cannot reach into a deep flower, like a rose. A flat dill or fennel head is easy for them to land on and feed from. Penn State Extension says this family draws in the tiny wasps and flies that lay eggs in garden pests. One study in Massachusetts found flowering fennel drew 48 kinds of egg-laying wasps. It also drew 8 kinds of hunting wasps, per Wisconsin Horticulture Extension. This family pulls in hoverflies too. Their larvae eat aphids.

Aster family (Asteraceae)

Cosmos · coneflower · coreopsis · blanketflower · sunflower · yarrow · goldenrod · tansy · signet marigold

Penn State Extension says this family draws bigger hunters. Ladybugs and soldier beetles like it best. These are cheap, easy flowers. Most gardeners already grow them. Plant them along the edge of your vegetable bed. Not just in a flower border. That is what brings the ladybugs over to your tomatoes.

Legumes (Fabaceae)

Alfalfa · fava bean · hairy vetch · sweet clover

These are usually grown as a cover crop, not as a garden flower. Penn State Extension says they draw many good bugs. If you already grow a cover crop, let a patch flower before you turn it under. That gives the good bugs a meal on the way.

Mustard family (Brassicaceae)

Sweet alyssum · basket-of-gold alyssum · mustard

Sweet alyssum is the easiest one on this list. It is a low plant. It flowers almost all the time. Wisconsin Horticulture Extension found its flowers draw large numbers of good bugs. One real caution from Penn State Extension: plant this family a little way from your vegetables. Not mixed in with them. The same plants that draw good bugs also draw pests.

Verbena family (Verbenaceae)

Lantana · Buenos Aires verbena · hybrid verbena

Penn State Extension says this family draws all kinds of good bugs. These are common garden-center flowers. An easy add to a pot or border you already have.

One more thing both sources agree on: plant a few of these so something is blooming from early spring to late fall. Not just one bed all at once in June. Good bugs need food the whole season, not one big meal.
